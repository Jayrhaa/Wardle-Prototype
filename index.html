What actually happened

Caesar had been drawn into the Egyptian civil war between Ptolemy XIII and his sister Cleopatra VII. When Mithridates of Pergamum arrived with a relief army, the combined force was roughly 20,000 strong. Facing it, the Egyptians held a strong position beside the Nile, with a fleet alongside.

Caesar's men took the fortified village in front of the camp, but attacks on the camp itself stalled because of the ground. With the defenders crowded toward the fighting, he noticed that the highest part of the camp had been left unguarded, since nature seemed to protect it. He sent several cohorts around to seize it, under an officer named Carfulenus.

Attacked from above and behind, the Egyptians broke. Many were crushed in the camp's ditch or drowned in the river, and Ptolemy drowned while fleeing. Accounts differ on exactly how.

The lesson in force deployment: pin the enemy at the front, then send a second force against the point he believes is safe.

Unit types, troop numbers and the map are a simplified reconstruction. Ancient sources do not give a precise map or reliable Egyptian numbers.
